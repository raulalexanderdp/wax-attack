# Chapter 2 — Part One: Chiba City Blues

*Nothing below reaches past the end of chapter 2.*

## What happened

The woman from the end of chapter 1 is Molly, and she isn't there to kill him.
She takes Case to the Chiba Hilton to meet her employer, Armitage.

Armitage has a file on Case, and it's a death sentence with a timetable: the
way he's living will kill him within a month, and if it somehow doesn't, his
pancreas goes inside a year. Then the offer — Armitage can undo the nerve
damage. Case can jack in again. In exchange, Case works for him as his console
jockey.

Armitage also hands over the first piece of the world's backstory: Screaming
Fist, a military operation in a war that ended some time before the novel
opens. The programs console cowboys use to crack banks are descended from
weapons built for it. Armitage was there.

Case takes the deal, because it was never really a deal. The clinic rebuilds
him. He gets new tissue, a new pancreas, and a wait: eight days before he can
jack in without destroying what was just repaired. There's a modification he
didn't ask for, too — his new pancreas neutralizes stimulants. The drugs don't
work on him any more.

Then the chapter turns. Case goes out for food, sees Linda Lee, and follows
her. Someone comes at him with a knife; Molly is there first. And Linda is
dead — killed, Molly tells him, over trying to fence the RAM she took from him,
on Julius Deane's word.

Part One ends there. Chiba is finished with him.

## What to notice

**The wish is granted, and that's the trap.** Everything Case wanted in chapter
1 he gets in chapter 2, and it arrives owned by someone else. This is the
novel's cleanest structural joke: the cure is also the leash. Watch how little
say he has at any point — he is diagnosed, priced, purchased, and repaired, and
the only question he's actually asked is one he can't answer no to.

**The drug modification is the tell.** Repairing his nerves buys his loyalty;
disabling the drugs removes his exit. Nobody explains it to him beforehand.
It's the moment to notice that Armitage isn't hiring Case so much as acquiring
him — and that the body Case wanted to escape has become the thing other people
edit.

**Molly is Case's opposite, and the book knows it.** He wants out of his flesh;
she has spent everything she has turning hers into equipment. The mirrored
lenses are the perfect image for the whole book — she can see you, you get your
own face back. That asymmetry, who can see whom, who knows what, is going to be
the shape of nearly every relationship in this novel.

**Armitage is a blank, deliberately.** Note how hard Gibson works to give him no
interior. He's handsome the way a rendering is handsome, and the prose keeps
sliding off him. That's not thin writing; it's characterization. File it.

**Screaming Fist is the book's first backward glance.** Gibson hands you a war
you didn't know had happened, in fragments, without a history lesson — the same
technique as the vocabulary in chapter 1. The technology has a military
parentage, which is a colder idea than it first looks: the tools of Case's art
were designed to hurt people.

**Linda Lee.** The chapter spends her. It's worth being a little suspicious of
how neatly that works — a woman killed offstage to give the male lead a wound
is one of the oldest moves in noir, and Gibson is running noir on purpose. What
saves it from being only that is how much room she goes on taking up in Case's
head. Watch that.

## Gibson's vocabulary

| term | what it means |
| --- | --- |
| razorgirl | the book's term for what Molly is: a body modified into a weapon, hired out |
| jockey | console jockey — a hacker on someone's payroll rather than freelance |
| Screaming Fist | a military operation in the past war; the ancestor of the cowboys' toolkit |
| the Chiba Hilton | the corporate hotel above Night City — the clean world sitting on the dirty one |

## Worth remembering

- Screaming Fist, and that Armitage was in it.
- How little personality Armitage has, and what that might be covering.
- The eight-day wait before Case can jack in.
- What Molly says she's in it for — and how much that explains.
- Julius Deane's hand in Linda's death.
- Linda Lee herself. She is not finished being part of this book.

## Where you are

End of chapter 2, end of Part One, 41 pages in. Chiba was the setup; the next
part moves. Part Two, The Shopping Expedition, runs five chapters, and the
title is doing exactly what it says — the crew gets assembled and the tools get
bought.
