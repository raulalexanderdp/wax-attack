Coverage Lighting — Day Exterior

REV 1, 16 OCT 2026  ·  SUN IS CAMERA LEFT ALL AFTERNOON
The method: subtract first, then your small sources matter. Nothing in the package out-guns an 8,700 fc sun. But cut the subject out of it with a solid and the ambient drops to roughly 800–1,500 fc — at which point an 8×8 bounce at 1,286 fc is the dominant key. You are not fighting the sun, you are removing it and replacing it with a key you chose the angle of.

Why this works on coverage and not on the wide: an overhead flown at 9 ft is in shot at 85 mm and clears frame from 135 mm up. The long lens hides the rig. That is the whole reason the tight pass is lightable and the 35 mm wide is not.

Hard silver is the only thing that competes, because it is re-aiming the sun. The HMI is four stops behind it — which is why it is a Set 2 unit, not a Set 1 unit.

A · CUT & BOUNCEVocalist, faces.
The workhorse.		6×6 solid overhead at 9 ft, 9.5 ft up-sun → subject out of direct sun. 8×8 ultrabounce camera left @ 10 ft = key, now dominant over ambient. 4×4 neg camera right sets the ratio. A 43° toplight becomes a ~30° soft side key you chose. Nothing bigger than an 8×8 in the air.
B · NATURAL + SUBTRACTVolume coverage.
5-minute build.		No overhead. Sun stays the 90° side key. 4×4 ultrabounce low @ 5 ft = eye light at −2.8 stops, which is exactly right to open the cap-brim shadow. 4×4 floppy sider for flare. 4×4 neg camera right to deepen the shadow side. Fast, repeatable, gets you shots per hour.
C · SHINY BOARDGuitar, hands,
strings, chrome.		6×6 solid overhead to cut, then 4×4 hard silver low camera left @ 15 ft — 3,538 fc, only 1.3 stops under sun. Turns a 43° toplight into a 15–20° raking key with real punch on an instrument. Do not put this in a singer's eyes. Details only.
